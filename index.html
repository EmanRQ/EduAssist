Red-flag checklist — how to spot a fake academic platform
  1. Value first, data second. Real tools answer, then maybe ask for an optional account. Fake ones demand a profile before showing anything.
  2. Fields that don't match the task. A homework solver never needs your matric number, phone, DOB or a security question.
  3. Urgency and scarcity. Countdowns, “only 3 slots left” and “verification expires” exist to stop you thinking.
  4. Verification that isn't verification. A real SSO redirect goes to your university domain, not a form on a random site.
  5. No policy, no contact, no owner. Look for a privacy page, a physical address and a domain registered before last week.

What you handed over

    How it gets used

    • Spear-phishing. Name + university + course context makes a fraudulent email nearly indistinguishable from a real one.
    • Credential stuffing. Security answers and reused PINs unlock email, banking and the student portal.
    • SIM swap / OTP theft. A phone number plus DOB is often enough to convince a telco support agent.
    • Identity fraud. Matric number + DOB + name is a usable identity bundle on campus systems.
    • Social proof farming. One “verified” student lends credibility to the next wave of scams.
